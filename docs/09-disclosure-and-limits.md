# Public portfolio boundaries and limitations

## Origins and independence

This repository is an **original synthetic construction**. It does not reuse private source code, taxonomy data files, original category IDs, original business product references, source-system credentials, enterprise architecture addresses, confidential documents, real employee names or customer/supplier information. Names, descriptions, numbers and events were newly created for this demo.

The category families are intentionally common to the generic painting and decorating trade and are expressed in English. The hierarchy is an independently authored example that demonstrates the domain pattern; it is not a verbatim export of a private taxonomy. Matching a business problem or a common architectural pattern does **not** imply that an employer sponsored or approved the published demo.

The program is suitable to demonstrate a Product Owner's *understanding* of product data, stakeholder responsibilities, governance and technical trade-offs. It should not be presented as historical evidence of work delivered to an employer or a deployed enterprise platform.

## Delivered demo features

UI, local persistence, synthetic products, taxonomy, attribute definitions, class-dependent values, source mappings, change requests, approval/rejection, version checking, audit records, explainable synthetic observations, read-only intelligence view and automated tests.

## Not production-ready

- No authenticated company identities or real authorization checks; `actor` and `reviewer` are user-entered simulation fields.
- No external ERP, warehouse, supplier or financial integration.
- No SSO, tenant isolation, per-field access policy, protected service identity or secret vault.
- SQLite is local demo persistence; production operations would require secure database infrastructure and migration management.
- Audit records are ordinary local database rows, not tamper-evident externally archived evidence.
- Taxonomy and metadata maintenance actions are simulated administration endpoints, not a comprehensive structural change-approval process.
- No accepted enterprise business glossary or operational source reconciliation has been performed.
- Analytical rules are illustrative and lack approved commercial thresholds, detailed order-allocation logic, time-bucketed demand, verified units or retained evaluation histories.
- No load/performance benchmark, disaster recovery test, independent security review or production user acceptance.

For safe publication, review the public contents and your own agreements concerning professional confidentiality and IP ownership before pushing any material based on professional experience.
